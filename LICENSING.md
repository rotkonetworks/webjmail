# Licensing

webjmail is **dual-licensed**, the same model [Stalwart](https://stalw.art) uses.

## 1. Open source — AGPL-3.0-only (default)

For everyone, webjmail is free and open source under the
[GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 only](./LICENSE)
(`SPDX-License-Identifier: AGPL-3.0-only`).

You may use, self-host, study, modify, and redistribute it. The catch that
protects the project: if you **modify webjmail and make it available to others
over a network**, AGPL section 13 requires you to offer those users the
**complete corresponding source** of your modified version. That keeps hosted
forks honest — improvements come back to the commons instead of disappearing
into a closed SaaS.

## 2. Commercial — webjmail Enterprise License

If you cannot or do not want to comply with the AGPL (for example, you want to
embed webjmail in a proprietary product or offer it as a managed service without
publishing your changes), Rotko Networks offers a separate commercial
[Enterprise License](./LICENSE-ENTERPRISE.txt) that waives the AGPL
obligations under a signed agreement.

Contact **hq@rotko.net** for terms.

## Why this works (and why the GitHub mirror can lag)

Rotko Networks holds the copyright to webjmail, so:

- **Only third parties are bound by the AGPL.** As the copyright holder, Rotko
  is not bound by the copyleft it grants others, and runs its own hosted build
  (app.webjmail.com) under its own terms — not under the AGPL. That is why the
  public GitHub source may trail the hosted/desktop build; the newest work can
  stay private for a while and is released to GitHub under AGPL on a delay.
- **Rotko can sell exceptions.** Because Rotko owns the copyright, it can offer
  the Enterprise License above to anyone — including selling a commercial
  license to another vendor — without affecting the public AGPL release.

Contributions from outside Rotko may require a contributor agreement so this
dual-licensing remains possible; if you plan to contribute, ask first.
